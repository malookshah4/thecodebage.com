This page answers the four percentage questions people actually run into: what is X% of a number, what percent one number is of another, how much something went up or down in percent, and what you get after adding or taking off a percentage. Each answer shows the working underneath, so you can check it or copy the method.

It is handy for discounts in a sale, a salary raise, tax on a bill, exam marks, or working out how much a price really jumped since last year.

## How to use

1. Find the box that matches your question. Each one works on its own.
2. **What is X% of a number?** Type the **Percent (%)** and the number in **Of**. The answer appears on the right.
3. **X is what percent of Y?** Enter the part in **X** and the whole in **Y**.
4. **Percentage increase or decrease** Enter the old value in **From** and the new value in **To**. The label changes to **Increase** or **Decrease** to match.
5. **Add or subtract a percentage** Enter the **Amount** and the **Percent (%)**. You get both results side by side.

All fields come prefilled with an example, so just type over them.

## The formulas behind each box

**Percent of a number** is percent ÷ 100 × number. 15% of 2,400 is 0.15 × 2,400 = 360.

**What percent X is of Y** is X ÷ Y × 100. If you scored 360 out of 2,400, that is 15%.

**Percentage change** is (new − old) ÷ old × 100. A price that went from 1,200 to 1,500 rose by 300, and 300 ÷ 1,200 is 0.25, so that is a 25% increase. Always divide by the old value, not the new one.

**Adding or removing a percentage** multiplies by (1 + p ÷ 100) or (1 − p ÷ 100). Adding 18% to 5,000 gives 5,000 × 1.18 = 5,900. Taking 18% off gives 5,000 × 0.82 = 4,100.

## Mistakes that trip people up

The most common one: a 20% rise followed by a 20% drop does not bring you back to where you started. Start at 100, add 20% to get 120, then take 20% off 120 and you land on 96. The second percentage is applied to a bigger number.

Another is removing tax the wrong way. If a price of 1,180 already includes 18% tax, the price before tax is not 1,180 minus 18%. It is 1,180 ÷ 1.18 = 1,000. Taking 18% off 1,180 gives 967.60, which is wrong.

People also mix up percent and percentage points. If an interest rate goes from 10% to 12%, it rose by 2 percentage points, but by 20% in relative terms. Banks and news reports often use whichever sounds better.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percent by 100 and multiply by the number. For 15% of 2,400, that is 0.15 × 2,400 = 360. The first box does this for you.

### How do I work out percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. Use the **Percentage increase or decrease** box and it shows the working.

### How do I find a price before a discount?

If you paid 800 after a 20% discount, divide by 0.8 to get 1,000. Dividing works here because the discount was taken from the original price, not the final one.
