Images to PDF turns photos and screenshots into a single PDF, one image per page. The usual reason is paperwork: you photographed your CNIC, a degree or a signed form, and the portal only accepts a PDF.

It runs in your browser. The images are not uploaded; the PDF is built on your own device and saved straight to your downloads.

## How to use

1. Tap **Choose images or drop them here** and pick your JPG, PNG or WebP files. You can add more at any time.
2. Put them in order with the up and down arrows. The small preview helps you spot the wrong photo. Remove extras with the X button.
3. Pick a **Page size**: A4, Letter, or **Fit to image** if you want each page to match its photo exactly.
4. Pick an **Orientation**. Auto turns a page sideways when the photo is wider than it is tall.
5. Pick a **Margin**. None fills the page, Small leaves a thin white border, Large leaves room for punching holes or stapling.
6. Leave **Compress large photos** on unless you need full resolution.
7. Name the file and press **Make PDF**.

## A4, Letter or fit to image

Use A4 in Pakistan, the Gulf, the UK, Europe and most of Asia. It is the standard paper size almost everywhere outside North America, and what an office printer in Lahore or Dubai will have loaded. Letter is the US and Canada size, a little wider and shorter. For a US company or portal, Letter is safer.

Fit to image makes each page the shape of its photo, with no white bands. Great on screen, odd on paper, because the printer has to scale an unusual page size. If it might get printed, pick A4 or Letter.

## Why compression is on by default

A modern phone camera takes photos of 12 megapixels or more, and each one is often 3 to 6 MB. Ten of those in a PDF can easily pass 40 MB, which many email services and upload forms will refuse. With compression on, any large photo is scaled so its longer side is 2000 pixels and saved as a good quality JPG. On an A4 page that is still about 240 dots per inch, which is sharper than most office printers can show, and small text in a document photo stays readable.

Turn it off for artwork or anything you will print large. Images already under about 1 MB are kept as they are either way.

## Getting clean document photos

Most bad PDFs come from the photo, not the converter. A few habits help:

* Shoot in daylight near a window, and turn off the flash. Flash makes a shiny spot right over the text.
* Hold the phone flat above the page, not at an angle, so the page stays rectangular.
* Put the paper on a dark table so the edges are easy to see.
* Crop in your gallery app before adding the photo here. The tool does not crop.

Photos taken sideways are turned upright using the rotation your phone stored in the file, so they appear the same way they do in your gallery.

## FAQ

### Is it free, and are my photos uploaded?
It is free and there is no account. Your photos stay on your device; the PDF is made inside your browser.

### How do I make a PDF from photos on my phone?
Open this page in Chrome or Safari, tap the box, and choose photos from your gallery. Press Make PDF and the file lands in your downloads, ready to attach.

### Can I convert HEIC photos from an iPhone?
When you pick photos through Safari, iOS usually converts HEIC to JPG for you. If a file is still rejected, change the camera format to "Most Compatible" in Settings, or export the photo as JPG first.

### Why is my PDF still large?
Either compression is off, or the images were PNG screenshots that are already under the size limit. Turn compression on, or convert big PNG files to JPG with an image compressor first.

### Can I make a PDF with several images on one page?
Not yet. Each image becomes its own page.
