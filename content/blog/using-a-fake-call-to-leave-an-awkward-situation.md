Everyone has been stuck somewhere they wanted to leave. A meeting that should have ended forty minutes ago. A salesperson who won't take no. A first date that went flat in the first five minutes. A neighbor who has a lot to say about their gutters.

The fake phone call is an old trick for this, and it works because it gives everyone a polite story. You don't have to say "I'm bored" or "this is uncomfortable." Your phone rings, you look at it, you say "sorry, I need to take this," and you're out. Nobody loses face.

I make an app that does this, so I've spent more time than is normal thinking about what makes a fake call believable. Here's what I've learned, plus a section at the end on what to do when the situation isn't just awkward but actually feels unsafe, because that's a different problem.

## When a fake call makes sense

A fake call is a social tool. It's for situations where you're not in danger, you'd just rather be somewhere else, and a direct exit would be rude or uncomfortable.

Some common ones:

- **Meetings that won't end.** Especially the kind where your part was done half an hour ago.
- **Pushy sales pitches.** In a shop, at your door, at a timeshare presentation you somehow ended up at.
- **Dates that aren't working.** Not unsafe, just clearly not going anywhere, and you'd like to leave politely.
- **Conversations you can't escape.** At a party, at a family event, at the gym. The person who keeps talking and doesn't notice you stepping back.
- **Being roped into something.** Someone is about to ask you to help them move house this weekend and you can see it coming.

In all of these the goal is the same: give yourself a reason to step away that doesn't need explaining.

## Set it up before you need it

The biggest mistake is trying to start a fake call in the middle of the awkward moment. Taking out your phone and tapping around for twenty seconds, then having it ring two seconds later, is not convincing. People notice.

The better approach is to schedule it ahead of time. If you're walking into a meeting you suspect will drag, set a call for 30 or 45 minutes in. If you're meeting someone for a first date, set one for 20 minutes after you sit down. If things are going well, you just decline it and carry on. If not, you have your exit.

For quick escapes, a short delay works too. Most fake call apps let you pick something like 15 seconds or 1 minute. Tap it while you're still putting your phone back in your pocket, then wait. A minute feels long when you're stuck, but it looks much more natural than an instant ring.

## Make the call look real

A few small details make a big difference.

**Pick a believable caller.** "Mom," "Boss," a colleague's first name, "Home," a name of a friend that the person you're with won't know. Avoid joke names. If someone glances at your screen and sees "Get Me Out Of Here," your cover is gone. Adding a photo for the contact helps too, since real contacts usually have one.

**Match the caller to the excuse.** If you plan to say "my sister needs a ride," then the caller should be your sister, not "Work." Think about this for two seconds before you set it up.

**Use a voice clip.** This is the detail most people skip. When you answer a fake call and there's silence, you have to fake the whole conversation, and if anyone is close enough to hear, it's obvious nothing is coming out of the speaker. Recording a short voice clip ahead of time (a friend saying "hey, are you free? I need you for a minute," or just some mumbled talking) means there's actual sound when you answer. Keep the volume low, like a normal call.

**Keep your phone on ring, not silent.** A fake call on a phone you always keep on silent is a bit suspicious if the people around you know you. And if the phone is on silent, you might miss your own escape. Pick a ringtone that sounds like your usual one.

**Act it out, briefly.** Look at the screen, make a small face, say "sorry, I have to take this," and step away before you answer. Then keep it vague: "Okay. Okay. Yeah, I can come now." Don't invent a huge drama. A simple "something came up" is more believable than a story with five details.

## A little etiquette

A fake call is a polite white lie, and like most white lies it works best when you don't overuse it.

If you use it to leave every meeting you find boring, people will notice the pattern. If you use it to skip things you've actually committed to, like a shift you agreed to cover or a friend's thing you promised to attend, that's not escaping an awkward moment, that's dodging a responsibility. The fake call doesn't change what you owe people.

Also, sometimes the honest exit is better. "I need to head out, it was nice talking to you" is a full sentence, and most adults accept it. I think of the fake call as the backup for when a direct exit feels harder than it should, not the default.

## If you actually feel unsafe

This part matters more than everything above.

A fake call is for awkward. It is **not** a safety tool. If you feel genuinely unsafe, whether on a date, with a stranger, walking home or anywhere else, don't rely on a fake call to get you out. A pretend conversation doesn't bring anyone to help you.

Instead:

1. **Call your local emergency number.** That's 911 in the US and Canada, 112 across the EU (and it works on many phones in other countries too), and 999 in the UK. If you're somewhere else, learn your local number before you need it. You can call even if you're not sure it's "serious enough."
2. **Share your live location with someone you trust.** Both Google Maps and WhatsApp can share live location for a set time. Send it to a friend or family member and tell them where you are and who you're with.
3. **Use your phone's built-in emergency SOS.** Android phones have an Emergency SOS feature (usually triggered by pressing the power button several times quickly, depending on your phone) that can call emergency services and share your location with emergency contacts. iPhones have Emergency SOS too. Set it up now, in your phone's settings, and add your emergency contacts while you're calm.
4. **Go somewhere with people.** A shop, a restaurant, a reception desk. Tell staff you need help. Many bars and venues are used to this.
5. **Call a real person.** An actual friend on an actual call is far better than a fake one. Ask them to stay on the line while you leave.

If you take one thing from this post, let it be this one.
