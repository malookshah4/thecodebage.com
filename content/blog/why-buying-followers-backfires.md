I'll be upfront about something awkward. One of my apps is called "For you - Follower, AI Enhance", and because of that name, people sometimes assume it sells followers. It doesn't. And I don't think anyone should buy them, from anyone. This post explains why, and what I'd do instead.

The pitch for bought followers is easy to understand. You're starting out, your account has 87 followers, and you see another account in your niche with 20,000. Some website offers 5,000 followers for a few dollars. It feels like a shortcut to looking credible. The problem is that it doesn't stay a shortcut for long.

## What you're actually buying

The followers you get from these services are almost never real people who chose to follow you. They're usually one of these:

- Bot accounts made in bulk, often with no profile picture, a random username, and zero posts.
- Old abandoned accounts that were hacked or bought and are now used to follow whoever paid.
- Accounts in "follow farms" where real people are paid tiny amounts to follow thousands of accounts they'll never look at.

None of these will watch your videos, like your posts, comment, share, or buy anything. They're numbers. And the number sits on your profile while the rest of your stats tell a different story.

## Your engagement rate collapses

This is the math most people don't think about before buying.

Say you have 1,000 real followers and your posts usually get around 80 likes and a handful of comments. Then you buy 9,000 fake followers. Now you have 10,000 followers and your posts still get about 80 likes, because bots don't like things. Before, your engagement looked healthy for your size. After, it looks like nobody cares about your content.

That matters because platforms look at how people respond to what you post. When the algorithm shows your video to a slice of your followers and almost nobody interacts, that's a signal that the content isn't interesting. Nobody outside these companies knows exactly how each ranking system works, and they change often. But it's widely understood that engagement from the people who see a post affects how far it spreads. Filling your follower list with accounts that never engage works against you.

Some services also sell fake likes and fake views to cover this up. That just stacks one fake signal on another, and the gap between your numbers and your real audience keeps growing.

## Platforms purge fake accounts

Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and the rest all remove fake and spam accounts regularly. Sometimes in big waves. When that happens, your bought followers disappear and your count drops, sometimes overnight. Many of these services know this and advertise a "refill guarantee", which tells you everything about how long their followers last.

A follower count that suddenly drops by thousands is also very visible. Anyone checking your account with a third-party analytics tool can see the spike when you bought, and the cliff when the platform cleaned up.

## Brands check

If your goal is sponsorships or brand deals, bought followers are close to the worst thing you can do.

Brands and agencies that pay creators have learned to look past follower counts. They look at engagement rate, at comments (are they real sentences or just emoji spam from random accounts?), and at audience data. Many use tools that estimate how much of an audience looks fake or suspicious, and where the audience is located. If you're a creator in one country and most of your followers seem to be from somewhere unrelated, with no real interaction, that's a red flag.

A small account with real, engaged followers is often more attractive to a brand than a big account with a dead audience. Smaller brands especially care about whether people actually listen to you.

## It breaks the rules and risks your account

Buying followers, likes or views goes against the terms of service of every major platform I know of. The consequences range from the fake engagement being removed, to reduced reach, to the account being restricted or suspended. You probably won't get banned for one small purchase. But you're putting the account you've worked on at risk for numbers that don't help you.

## The password scams

This part makes me genuinely angry, because it hurts people who are just trying to grow.

A lot of "free followers" sites and apps ask you to log in with your social media username and password. Some of them just steal the account. Others keep access and use your account to follow, like and spam on behalf of their paying customers, so you become part of the bot network. Some lock you out and ask for money to give it back.

The rule is simple: never give your password to a third-party site or app that promises followers. If you've done it already, change your password, turn on two-factor authentication, and check your account's list of logged-in devices and connected apps.

## What actually works

None of this is exciting, and none of it is fast. But this is what I've seen help real accounts grow.

**Post consistently.** Not every hour, just regularly. A schedule you can actually keep, like three times a week, beats posting ten times in one week and then nothing for a month. Consistency also gives you more chances to learn what your audience responds to.

**Make your videos look better.** Viewers decide quickly. A dark, blurry, shaky video gets skipped even when the idea is good. Simple fixes go a long way: face a window for natural light, wipe your phone's camera lens (seriously, it's often smudged), lock exposure, and hold the phone steady or use a cheap tripod.

**Use relevant hashtags, not 30 random ones.** Stuffing the maximum number of popular tags rarely helps. A few specific, relevant tags that match what the video is about are more useful, along with one or two broader ones. Trending tags help only when your video actually fits them.

**Pick a niche.** It's much easier for people to decide to follow you when they know what they'll get. "Cheap home workouts", "beginner guitar", "street food in Lahore". You can branch out later.

**Reply to comments.** Especially early on. People notice when a creator answers, and replies keep the conversation going under your post. I wrote a separate piece on [how to reply to comments without sounding like a bot](/blog/how-to-reply-to-comments-without-sounding-like-a-bot/) if that part feels awkward.

**Collaborate.** Duets, stitches, joint videos, shoutouts with creators around your size. Real collaborations put you in front of real people who already like similar content.

**Work on the opening of your videos.** Most viewers leave in the first few seconds if nothing grabs them. My notes on [the first three seconds of a short video](/blog/the-first-three-seconds-of-a-short-video/) go through this in detail.
