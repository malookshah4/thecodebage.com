You post a video. Forty comments come in. You reply "Thanks" to twenty of them, heart the rest, and go to bed. It feels like engagement. To the people who wrote those comments, it reads like a vending machine.

Replying to comments is one of the cheapest ways to grow a small audience, and most creators do it badly, me included at first. Not because they don't care, but because replying to a pile of comments is tiring, so the replies get shorter and shorter until they mean nothing. This post is about doing it in a way that actually sounds like you, even when you're tired, and even if you use AI to help.

## Why bother replying at all

Two reasons, and they're both pretty simple.

The first is people. Someone took ten seconds out of their day to say something to you. When you answer them like a person, there's a decent chance they come back, and a smaller chance they become one of the regulars who comment on everything. Those regulars are your community. You don't build one with heart emojis.

The second is the algorithm, sort of. Platforms like YouTube, TikTok and Instagram look at engagement, and a back and forth conversation in the comments is engagement. Nobody outside those companies knows the exact weights, and I'm suspicious of anyone who claims to. But a comment section with real conversation makes the post look alive to the next viewer too, and that part I'm sure about.

## Show up in the first hour

If you can, stay around for the first hour after you post. Early comments are the ones most people will see, because they tend to sit near the top. Replying to them fast does three things: it tells the commenter you're actually there, it gives later viewers a conversation to join, and it gives you a quick read on how the post is landing.

You don't have to answer everything in that hour. Pick the comments that say something. A real question, a joke you can play off, a strong opinion. Those are worth your time. "First" is not.

If you can't be there, that's fine. Come back later and reply to the good ones even if it's a day late. A late real reply beats a fast empty one.

## Reply to what they actually said

This is the whole trick, really. Look at the specific thing in the comment and respond to that thing.

Say someone writes "the part at 0:42 where the cat knocks over the cup killed me." A bot reply is "Haha thanks for watching" A human reply is "She did that three times before I got it on camera, the cup didn't survive." The second one has a detail only you could know. It shows you read it.

Some patterns that help:

- **Quote back a detail.** Mention the timestamp, the specific step, the thing they noticed.
- **Add one small fact** they didn't know. A behind the scenes bit, the mistake you made, the tool you used.
- **Ask a question back.** "Did you try it with the smaller size?" or "Which part tripped you up?" Questions keep the thread going and the answers often teach you something.
- **Use their name** if it fits. Not every time, or it gets creepy.

Keep replies short. Two sentences is usually enough. You're chatting, not writing an essay.

## Dealing with negative comments

Not every comment will be nice. I think it helps to sort them into a few buckets in your head.

**Trolls.** Someone who's clearly trying to get a reaction, with nothing real behind it. Ignore them. Don't reply, don't argue, and use hide or block tools if they keep going. Replying gives them exactly what they came for and pushes their comment higher.

**Fair criticism.** "Your audio is really quiet in this one." "Step 3 is wrong, it should be the other way round." These are gifts, even when they sting. Answer calmly, thank them if they're right, and fix it next time. "You're right, I messed up the mic gain, fixed for the next one" makes you look better, not worse.

**Rude but with a point.** This is the tricky middle. Someone says something harsh, but there's a real issue in there. I usually reply only to the point and ignore the tone. You don't have to accept the insult to accept the feedback.

What I'd avoid in every case: arguing in public. Even if you're right, a long back and forth in your comments looks bad, and you'll spend an evening on it that you could have spent making the next video.

## Pin the good stuff, and mine it for ideas

Most platforms let you pin a comment to the top. Use it. Pin a comment that adds something: a correction, a funny reaction, a useful question with your answer. It sets the tone for everyone who scrolls down after. Pinning a thoughtful comment also tends to encourage more thoughtful comments.

Your comments are also the best content idea list you'll ever get, for free. If three people ask "how did you do the transition at the end," that's your next video. When you post it, go back and reply to those people with a link. They asked, you answered with a whole video. That's how regulars are made.

I keep a simple note on my phone and paste in any question that comes up more than once. When I run out of ideas, I open that note.

## If you use AI to help with replies

I'm not against using AI for this. When you have 200 comments and limited energy, a tool that drafts a few options can help you get unstuck. But there are some rules I'd stick to.

**Always edit the suggestion.** Treat it as a rough draft. Change a word, add the detail only you know, cut the part that sounds like a greeting card. If you send it exactly as generated, people can often tell.

**Keep your voice.** If you never use the word "awesome" in real life, delete it. If you write in lowercase with no punctuation, keep doing that. Your audience knows how you talk.

**Kill the generic lines.** "Thanks for watching!" "Glad you enjoyed it." "Great question." These are the replies that make a comment section feel automated, whether a human or a bot wrote them. If the suggestion opens with one of these, cut it and start from the second sentence.

**Never auto-post.** Don't connect anything that replies for you without you reading it first. AI can misread sarcasm, miss that a comment is actually about something sad, or reply cheerfully to a troll. One bad automated reply can do more damage than a hundred good ones fix.

**Don't fake a conversation.** Using AI to help phrase a reply is fine. Using it to pretend you watched someone's video or know something you don't is not.
